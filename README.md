# Project Cold Meadow

### Understanding Organizational Structure Through Computational Analysis

> *Some organizations thrive.*
>
> *Some dissolve naturally.*
>
> *Some become associated with documented harm.*
>
> **What organizational characteristics distinguish them?**

---

## Overview

Project Cold Meadow is a computational social science research project exploring the relationship between organizational structure and documented harmful outcomes in historical high-control organizations.

Using publicly available historical records and structured datasets, the project examines whether recurring organizational characteristics appear across organizations that experienced documented violence, fraud, coercive control, or other harmful outcomes.

Rather than attempting to classify organizations or predict future events, Cold Meadow investigates historical case studies to better understand recurring structural patterns through transparent, reproducible data analysis.

---

## Research Question

**Which organizational characteristics consistently appear across historical high-control organizations with documented harmful outcomes?**

Examples include:

- Leadership structure
- Organizational hierarchy
- Geographic isolation
- Financial dependence
- Information control
- Exit barriers
- Recruitment strategies
- Internal governance
- Organizational growth
- External conflict

The objective is to explore measurable associations—not establish causation or make predictive claims.

---

## Motivation

History offers numerous examples of organizations that evolved in dramatically different ways despite sharing similar structural characteristics.

Some remained peaceful.

Some dissolved naturally.

Others became associated with documented violence, financial exploitation, abuse, or coercive control.

Project Cold Meadow explores whether these historical organizations exhibit recurring organizational patterns that can be studied computationally rather than relying solely on anecdotal observation.

The project grew from an interest in organizational behavior, criminology, and forensic research, with the goal of applying modern data science techniques to historical case studies.

## Methodology

Project Cold Meadow follows a reproducible research workflow.

### Phase 1 — Develop a Coding Framework

Organizational characteristics will be defined using a standardized coding methodology.

Example variables include:

- Leadership centralization
- Communal living
- Geographic isolation
- Financial control
- Recruitment methods
- Information control
- Internal disciplinary systems
- Exit barriers
- Organizational size
- Years active
- Organizational outcome

---

### Phase 2 — Historical Data Collection

Information will be collected from publicly available sources, including:

- Academic publications
- Government reports
- Court records
- Historical archives
- Books
- Investigative journalism
- Public documentaries

Every variable will be traceable to documented source material whenever possible.

---

### Phase 3 — Dataset Construction

Qualitative historical information will be transformed into structured analytical datasets.

Example:

| Organization | Leadership Centralization | Geographic Isolation | Financial Control | Documented Harm |
|--------------|--------------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Example | High | Moderate | High | Yes |

- Network Analysis
- Feature Importance
- Data Visualization

The emphasis is on understanding patterns rather than building predictive models.

## Ethics

Project Cold Meadow analyzes documented historical organizations using publicly available information.

The project does **not** attempt to:

- classify contemporary organizations,
- evaluate religious beliefs,
- predict future organizational behavior,
- diagnose psychological conditions,
- or make legal determinations.

Historical case studies are examined solely to explore recurring organizational characteristics using transparent and reproducible analytical methods.

Observed relationships should be interpreted as associations within the dataset rather than evidence of causation.
